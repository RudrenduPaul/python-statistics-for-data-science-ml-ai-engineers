## Addendum: what/why/how narrative and rigor pass (2026-09-01)

Audited the chapter for overstated confidence and hidden limitations; the prior visual-QA pass
above confirmed narrative completeness was thorough, so this pass focused on rigor. Two fixes,
no narrative gaps found:

1. **Overclaim, Ridge/Lasso closed-form posteriors**: the transition into the PyMC section stated
   flatly that "Ridge and Lasso have closed-form Bayesian posteriors." Only Ridge does: its
   Gaussian prior is conjugate to the Gaussian likelihood. The Bayesian Lasso's Laplace prior is
   not conjugate, so only its posterior mode (the point estimate) has a closed form; the full
   posterior needs Park and Casella's Gibbs sampler, the same paper this chapter cites for the
   Ridge/Lasso-as-priors equivalence. Rewrote the transition to state this correctly, using no
   new citation.
2. **Omitted limitation, horseshoe prior**: the horseshoe section described its shrinkage
   behavior and said fitting it costs "added computation" over the Lasso without saying what that
   computation buys against. Added a sentence naming the mechanism: the horseshoe's local and
   global shrinkage weights create a funnel-shaped posterior that plain MCMC struggles to
   explore, which is why PyMC fits it with a non-centered parameterization. Confirmed this is
   standard MCMC-diagnostics knowledge (Neal's funnel, Piironen and Vehtari's horseshoe-sampling
   literature), not a single-source claim, so no citation was added.

Sections reviewed with no change needed: the conjugate-posterior derivation and its worked
example, the credible-vs-confidence-interval demonstration, the Ridge/Lasso/horseshoe shrinkage
comparison itself, the PyMC logistic-regression code and convergence-diagnostics subsection, and
the posterior predictive check. Re-checked for em dashes and the project's banned precision-word
